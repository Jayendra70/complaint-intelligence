# Target Decision Memo: Defining "Escalation Risk"

## Background
A core objective of the platform is predicting whether an incoming complaint is
at risk of "escalating" — a high-friction case that consumes resources or ends
badly. The CFPB dataset used to provide a direct label (`Consumer disputed?`),
but it was discontinued in April 2017, and modern exports (including our
2023-03 snapshot) do not carry the field at all.

## Decision: proxy label on modern data (Option A)

We define, on the narrative subset:

```
is_escalated = 1  if company_response_to_consumer == "Closed with monetary relief"
               or company_response_to_consumer == "Untimely response"
               or timely_response == "No"
```

Rows with response `"In progress"` are excluded — their outcome is not yet known.

**Implementation note:** matching is by exact category, not substring. A naive
`"monetary relief" in response` also matches *"Closed with NON-monetary
relief"* — on our real data that single bug would inflate the positive rate
from **3.1% to ~35%** and silently poison every downstream model.

## Measured prevalence (2023-03 snapshot, 26,285 narratives)

| Signal | Count | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Closed with monetary relief | 663 | 2.5% |
| Untimely (either field) | 165 | 0.6% |
| **is_escalated = 1 (union)** | **819** | **3.1%** |

After dedup the train-split positive rate is **4.1%** — a genuinely imbalanced
target, which is exactly what makes the modeling honest: PR-AUC, calibrated
probabilities and threshold tuning (Phase 2), not accuracy.

## Why not the pre-2017 slice (Option B)?
Training on 2015–2017 data with the real `Consumer disputed?` flag was
considered. Rejected as the primary path because (a) the language of complaints
(products, apps, vocabulary) has shifted materially since 2017, (b) the
deployment story targets current data, and (c) our snapshot lacks the field
entirely. Option B remains a good robustness check once the bulk download is in
place: train the same architecture on the pre-2017 slice and compare.

## Tradeoffs, stated plainly
- **Pro:** modern vocabulary and products; business-real framing ("cases that
  cost money or break SLA"); label available on every resolved complaint.
- **Con:** it is an approximation — the union conflates two different phenomena
  (consumer *won* money vs company *missed* the deadline). A furious consumer
  whose case closed "with explanation" on time is a 0 under this proxy.
- **Con:** monetary relief is decided *after* reading the complaint; the model
  predicts the eventual outcome from the narrative at submission time. The
  label-source fields are dropped from the processed features (leakage guard in
  `01_data_cleaning.ipynb`) so nothing about the resolution leaks into inputs.

Implemented in `notebooks/01_data_cleaning.ipynb`; prevalence figures above are

## Update (2026-07-14): component labels ship separately

Review feedback, accepted: the union mixes two linguistically different
outcomes — a narrative that reads like a valid claim (monetary relief) vs a
company missing its SLA (untimely), where the latter is driven largely by
company operations rather than complaint text. `01_data_cleaning.ipynb` now
writes three label columns:

| Column | Positives (2023-03 narrative subset) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `is_monetary_relief` | 663 (2.5%) | primary modeling target — plausibly text-predictable |
| `is_untimely` | 165 (0.6%) | too thin to model alone at one-month scale (~130 train positives); viable after the bulk download (~6k+ positives at 1M+ narratives) |
| `is_escalated` (union) | 819 (3.1%) | retained as a business/triage aggregate, not a modeling target |

Overlap between the two components is 9 rows, so this is a **multi-label**
(two-heads) problem, not multi-class — the classes are not mutually exclusive.
Phase 2 baselines will be trained and reported per-component, with the union
score derived from the component predictions if triage needs one number.

Related robustness note: company names appear un-redacted in narratives
(measured: ~34% of top-10-company complaints mention their own company; 65-84%
for the big banks). For `is_untimely` especially, a text model may shortcut to
company identity. Phase 3 error analysis includes a company-name-masking
ablation and per-company slice metrics to quantify this.
